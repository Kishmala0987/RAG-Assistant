# Employee HR Policies

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Employee HR Policy document establishes the general rules, procedures, and expectations that apply to all employees of the organization. The purpose of these policies is to provide a consistent and transparent framework for workplace conduct, attendance, compensation, leave, performance management, professional development, workplace safety, and employee responsibilities.

These policies apply to full-time employees, part-time employees, interns, trainees, and temporary employees unless a specific policy states otherwise. Certain departments or roles may have additional requirements because of the nature of their work. Where a departmental policy conflicts with a general HR policy, the applicable departmental policy should be reviewed with Human Resources.

Employees are expected to read and understand the policies applicable to their role. Failure to comply with company policies may result in corrective action, which can include verbal warnings, written warnings, suspension, or termination depending on the nature and severity of the violation.

## 2. Working Hours and Attendance

The standard working schedule for full-time employees is eight working hours per day, five days per week. Individual departments may establish different working schedules based on operational requirements. Employees will be informed of their assigned working hours when they join the organization.

Employees are expected to arrive at work on time and remain available during their scheduled working hours. Consistent lateness, unexplained absences, or leaving the workplace without authorization may be considered attendance violations.

Employees who are unable to attend work because of illness, emergency, or another legitimate reason should notify their immediate supervisor as soon as reasonably possible. Whenever possible, notification should be provided before the employee's scheduled start time.

Employees working remotely are expected to maintain the same level of availability and professionalism expected from employees working from the office. Remote employees should remain reachable through approved communication channels during their working hours and should attend scheduled meetings unless prior approval has been obtained.

## 3. Remote and Hybrid Work

The organization may permit eligible employees to work remotely or under a hybrid arrangement. Remote work is considered a work arrangement rather than an entitlement and may be modified or withdrawn based on business requirements, employee performance, security concerns, or operational needs.

Employees working remotely are responsible for maintaining a suitable workspace and ensuring that company information cannot be accessed by unauthorized individuals. Employees should use company-approved devices, communication platforms, authentication methods, and security controls whenever required.

Confidential documents should not be printed, downloaded, transferred to personal devices, or shared with other individuals unless such activity is necessary for legitimate business purposes and permitted by company policy.

Employees working remotely should maintain reliable communication during working hours. If an employee experiences an internet, electricity, hardware, or other technical problem that prevents them from working, they should inform their supervisor and provide an estimated recovery time.

## 4. Leave Policy

Employees may be eligible for annual leave, sick leave, casual leave, parental leave, and other forms of leave according to their employment agreement and applicable law.

Annual leave should normally be requested in advance through the company's approved leave management system. Employees should provide reasonable notice when planning extended periods of leave so that managers can arrange appropriate workload coverage.

Sick leave may be taken when an employee is unable to work because of illness or a medical condition. The company may request supporting documentation when required by applicable policy or law, particularly for extended periods of absence.

In cases of unexpected illness or emergency, employees should notify their supervisor as soon as reasonably possible. Employees should not assume that a leave request has been approved until they receive confirmation through the appropriate channel.

Managers are responsible for reviewing leave requests fairly and considering both the employee's circumstances and the operational requirements of the department.

## 5. Public Holidays

The organization observes public holidays according to the applicable national and regional holiday calendar. Employees required to work on an official holiday may receive compensation, time off in lieu, or another applicable benefit according to company policy and applicable employment regulations.

The HR department will communicate the official holiday schedule at the beginning of each calendar year or whenever changes occur.

## 6. Compensation and Payroll

Employees are compensated according to the salary and benefits specified in their employment agreement. Salaries are normally processed according to the organization's established monthly payroll schedule.

Employees are responsible for ensuring that HR has accurate banking, tax, identification, and other information required for payroll processing.

Any discrepancy in salary, deductions, allowances, or other payroll information should be reported to HR as soon as possible. HR will investigate reported discrepancies and provide clarification where necessary.

Salary information is considered confidential. Employees should avoid accessing, sharing, or disclosing another employee's compensation information unless such access is required as part of their professional responsibilities.

## 7. Overtime

Overtime may be required when business conditions or operational demands make additional working hours necessary. Employees should obtain approval from their manager before working overtime whenever reasonably possible.

Overtime compensation or time off in lieu will be handled according to the employee's employment agreement, company policy, and applicable law.

Employees should not regularly work excessive hours without discussing workload concerns with their manager. Managers are responsible for monitoring workloads and ensuring that overtime is managed appropriately.

## 8. Performance Management

The organization uses performance management processes to help employees understand expectations, receive feedback, identify development opportunities, and improve their contribution to the organization.

Performance may be evaluated using factors such as quality of work, productivity, reliability, communication, teamwork, problem-solving, professional conduct, achievement of assigned objectives, and compliance with organizational policies.

Managers should provide employees with regular feedback rather than waiting until a formal annual review. Employees are encouraged to discuss challenges, development goals, and performance expectations with their managers throughout the year.

Where performance does not meet expectations, the organization may establish a performance improvement plan. A performance improvement plan may define specific performance concerns, expected improvements, available support, measurable objectives, and a review period.

## 9. Promotion and Career Development

Promotions are based on factors including performance, demonstrated skills, experience, business requirements, leadership capability, and availability of suitable positions.

Employees interested in career development should discuss their goals with their manager or HR representative. The organization may provide training, mentoring, workshops, technical courses, leadership programs, or other development opportunities depending on business needs and available resources.

Participation in training does not automatically guarantee promotion or salary adjustment. Employees are expected to apply relevant knowledge and skills acquired through professional development activities.

## 10. Code of Conduct

Employees are expected to behave professionally and respectfully toward colleagues, customers, partners, visitors, and other individuals they interact with as part of their work.

Employees must perform their responsibilities honestly and in good faith. Misrepresentation of information, falsification of records, unauthorized use of company property, theft, fraud, or deliberate misuse of company systems may result in disciplinary action.

Employees should avoid behavior that could reasonably damage the reputation or legitimate interests of the organization.

Professional disagreements are expected to be handled respectfully. Employees should focus discussions on facts, responsibilities, and possible solutions rather than personal criticism.

## 11. Anti-Harassment and Respectful Workplace

The organization is committed to maintaining a workplace where employees are treated with dignity and respect.

Harassment, bullying, intimidation, discrimination, threats, unwanted conduct, or other inappropriate behavior is not acceptable.

Employees who experience or witness inappropriate workplace behavior are encouraged to report the matter through an appropriate reporting channel. Reports will be handled as confidentially as reasonably possible while allowing the organization to investigate the matter.

Retaliation against an employee who raises a good-faith concern or participates in an investigation is prohibited.

## 12. Equal Employment Opportunity

Employment decisions should be based on legitimate factors such as qualifications, skills, experience, performance, organizational requirements, and other job-related considerations.

The organization does not tolerate unlawful discrimination in recruitment, compensation, promotion, training, performance management, or termination.

Managers and employees are expected to support an inclusive professional environment in which individuals are treated fairly and respectfully.

## 13. Information Security

Employees must protect company information and systems from unauthorized access, loss, disclosure, or misuse.

Passwords must not be shared with other individuals. Employees should use strong authentication methods and follow company requirements for multi-factor authentication when available.

Company devices should be protected with appropriate passwords, security controls, and software updates. Employees should not install unauthorized software on company-managed devices.

Suspicious emails, unexpected attachments, unusual login notifications, potential phishing attempts, or other security incidents should be reported to the designated IT or security team.

Employees must not attempt to bypass security controls, access systems without authorization, or use another employee's credentials.

## 14. Confidentiality

Employees may have access to confidential information including customer information, business strategies, financial information, product plans, source code, research, internal communications, employee records, and other proprietary information.

Confidential information may only be accessed and used for legitimate business purposes. Employees must not disclose confidential information to unauthorized individuals during or after their employment.

When employment ends, employees must return company property and follow instructions regarding the deletion or return of confidential information stored on personal or company-managed devices.

## 15. Acceptable Use of Technology

Company technology resources are provided primarily for legitimate business activities. Employees may use company systems for limited personal purposes when such use does not interfere with work responsibilities, violate company policy, create security risks, or consume excessive resources.

Employees must not use company systems to access, distribute, or store illegal, harmful, discriminatory, or inappropriate material.

The organization may monitor the use of company systems where permitted by applicable law and company policy. Employees should not assume that information stored on company-managed systems is completely private.

## 16. Artificial Intelligence and Generative AI

Employees may use approved artificial intelligence tools to support legitimate business activities where such use is permitted by the organization.

Employees must not enter confidential customer information, private employee information, passwords, proprietary source code, unreleased business information, or other restricted data into external AI systems unless the organization has explicitly approved the relevant tool and use case.

AI-generated content must be reviewed by an employee before being used for important business decisions, customer communications, legal documents, financial decisions, or other high-impact activities.

Employees remain responsible for the accuracy, quality, confidentiality, and appropriateness of work produced with the assistance of AI tools.

AI tools must not be used to make unauthorized decisions about employees, customers, or other individuals.

## 17. Company Property

Company property includes computers, mobile devices, access cards, software licenses, documents, equipment, furniture, and other resources provided for work purposes.

Employees are responsible for taking reasonable care of company property assigned to them.

Company property should not be removed from the workplace, transferred to another person, or used for unauthorized purposes without appropriate permission.

Lost or stolen company equipment must be reported promptly to the appropriate department.

## 18. Expenses and Business Travel

Employees may be reimbursed for reasonable and authorized business expenses incurred while performing their responsibilities.

Employees should obtain approval for significant expenses before making purchases whenever required by company procedures.

Expense claims should include appropriate receipts and supporting documentation. False, exaggerated, or unauthorized expense claims may result in disciplinary action.

Business travel should be planned according to organizational travel procedures. Employees are expected to select reasonable transportation and accommodation options consistent with applicable travel guidelines.

## 19. Workplace Health and Safety

The organization is committed to providing a safe and healthy working environment.

Employees should follow workplace safety procedures and report hazards, accidents, injuries, or unsafe conditions promptly.

Emergency exits, fire safety equipment, first-aid resources, and other safety facilities must not be blocked or misused.

Employees should not engage in behavior that creates unnecessary risk to themselves or others.

## 20. Conflict of Interest

Employees should avoid situations in which personal interests could improperly influence their professional responsibilities.

A conflict of interest may arise when an employee has a financial interest, personal relationship, outside employment, or other involvement that could affect their ability to make impartial business decisions.

Employees should disclose potential conflicts to their manager or HR department as soon as they become aware of them.

Having a potential conflict does not automatically mean that an employee has violated company policy. The organization will review disclosed situations and determine appropriate steps to manage the conflict.

## 21. Gifts and Business Hospitality

Employees should not accept gifts, payments, favors, or entertainment that could improperly influence business decisions.

Small customary business gifts may be acceptable where permitted by company policy and applicable law. Employees should consult their manager or compliance representative when they are uncertain whether a gift or invitation is appropriate.

Employees must never accept or offer anything that could reasonably be interpreted as a bribe or improper payment.

## 22. Social Media

Employees are responsible for ensuring that their personal social media activity does not improperly disclose confidential company information or represent personal opinions as official statements of the organization.

Employees should not publish confidential customer information, internal documents, private employee information, or unreleased business information.

Only authorized representatives may speak publicly on behalf of the organization.

Employees should communicate respectfully when discussing workplace-related topics online.

## 23. Recruitment and Referral

Employees may refer qualified candidates for open positions through approved recruitment channels.

All candidates must be evaluated using consistent and job-related criteria. Employee referrals do not guarantee interviews, employment, or preferential treatment.

Employees involved in recruitment must disclose personal relationships or other conflicts that could affect the fairness of the hiring process.

## 24. Probation Period

New employees may be required to complete a probationary period as specified in their employment agreement.

During probation, the organization evaluates the employee's performance, conduct, attendance, skills, ability to work with the team, and suitability for the position.

Managers should provide appropriate feedback during the probation period. At the end of the probation period, the organization may confirm employment, extend the probation period where permitted, or end employment according to applicable policy and law.

## 25. Resignation and Notice Period

Employees who wish to resign should submit written notice according to the notice period specified in their employment agreement and applicable law.

During the notice period, employees are expected to continue performing their responsibilities professionally and support an orderly transition of their work.

Employees should return company property, complete required documentation, transfer relevant files and responsibilities, and cooperate with the organization's exit procedures.

## 26. Termination of Employment

Employment may end through resignation, completion of a fixed-term agreement, redundancy, retirement, or termination in accordance with applicable policies and law.

In cases involving serious misconduct, the organization may take immediate disciplinary action where permitted by applicable law.

Employees whose employment ends must return company property and comply with continuing obligations relating to confidentiality, intellectual property, and other applicable agreements.

## 27. Grievance Procedure

Employees who have concerns regarding workplace treatment, management decisions, compensation, working conditions, harassment, discrimination, or other employment matters may raise a grievance through the designated HR process.

Employees should provide sufficient information for the organization to understand and investigate the concern.

HR may meet with the employee, manager, witnesses, or other relevant individuals as part of the investigation.

Employees are expected to cooperate honestly with workplace investigations. Deliberately providing false information may result in disciplinary action.

## 28. Disciplinary Action

Disciplinary action is intended to address policy violations and provide employees with an opportunity to correct inappropriate behavior or performance where appropriate.

Depending on the circumstances, disciplinary measures may include counseling, verbal warnings, written warnings, suspension, performance improvement measures, or termination.

The seriousness of the violation, previous conduct, employee history, impact on the organization, and other relevant circumstances may be considered when determining appropriate action.

Serious misconduct may result in stronger disciplinary action without following every stage of progressive discipline.

## 29. Data Privacy

Employee and customer personal information must be handled responsibly and only for legitimate purposes.

Access to personal information should be limited to individuals who require it for their professional responsibilities.

Employees must follow applicable privacy laws, organizational security procedures, and data-handling requirements.

Personal information should not be copied, exported, stored, or shared unnecessarily.

## 30. Policy Updates

The organization may update HR policies when business requirements, technology, organizational structures, or applicable laws change.

Employees will be informed of significant policy changes through appropriate communication channels.

Employees are responsible for reviewing updated policies and complying with the latest applicable requirements.

## 31. Questions and Policy Interpretation

Employees who are uncertain about how a policy applies to a particular situation should contact Human Resources or their manager for clarification.

HR is responsible for providing guidance on the interpretation and application of HR policies. Where a policy conflicts with applicable law, the applicable legal requirements will take precedence.

Employees should not rely solely on informal interpretations of company policies when making decisions that could have significant employment, financial, legal, security, or operational consequences.

## 32. Employee Acknowledgment

By accepting employment with the organization, employees acknowledge that they are expected to familiarize themselves with applicable company policies and procedures.

Employees are responsible for seeking clarification when they do not understand a policy or requirement.

The organization expects all employees to contribute to a professional, respectful, secure, and productive workplace.
